How a run moves through the system

1. Submit

The dashboard sends an authenticated, idempotent batch request. PostgreSQL stores jobs before dispatch.

2. Dispatch

Vercel Queues delivers job IDs to private consumers. Repair dispatch can republish unfinished jobs.

3. Coordinate

Database leases, concurrency controls, daily allowances, and a shared model schedule coordinate workers.

4. Observe and act

The model chooses keys, pressure plates, and exits. A deterministic navigator applies identical moves to both builds.

5. Verify

An exhaustive search checks completion and route budgets. Incomplete play remains a separate outcome.

6. Recover and replay

Checkpoints preserve moves, observations, and usage. A replacement worker resumes; published links expose a result-only projection.

    What the game now tests

    Six levels and six updates form 36 labelled combinations. Key Management adds a persistent inventory state: collect the key, cross a locked door, trigger the pressure plate, and finish. A broken key pickup makes the candidate impossible to complete. A longer but solvable route can also violate a level's move allowance. The published scaling reports retain their original 20-case live and 25-case offline workloads.

    This is an instrumented grid-game testbed. Supporting arbitrary browser games would require new observation and execution adapters.
